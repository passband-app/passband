01 · DATA FLOW

One tenant. Every wire out of it.

Your hosted account is its own instance of the Passband daemon with its own database. Nothing else runs in it and no other account can reach it. These are the only ways data leaves.

We run the daemon for you. Every channel below is live. You run the daemon. Every channel we operate is off; what is left talks to Google, to your devices, and to a model provider only if you give it your own key.

04 · CONTROLS

How it is held.

The short version of the threat model. The long one is public.

Reading can't send

Sync loads its own credential slot. The one used to archive, label and send lives apart, and the sync path cannot load it.

One instance per account

Your own container and volume, user-namespaced, behind a default-deny network policy. Tenants cannot reach each other.

Keys you can count

Your Google token is sealed to a key only your tenant holds. Cluster secrets are encrypted at rest. Backups are encrypted before they leave the server.

Login codes are sealed

Verification codes, sign-in links and password resets are sealed as they are recognized, by rules first and triage second. Sealed mail is never shown to the assistant or to AI agents, and opens only when you reveal it.

Agents read, never write

The interface Passband offers to AI agents is read-only and serves text only, never HTML and never sealed mail.

Mail renders sandboxed

Email is sanitized when it arrives and shown with scripts off, navigation blocked and nothing written to disk.

What we do not have yet

Said here so you do not have to find it in a questionnaire. No SOC 2 report. Hosted tenants run in one region on one server, and the mailbox volume is not encrypted at the disk layer; the Google token and the backups are. The people who run Passband can, in principle, reach a hosted mailbox, and do so only to answer a request you made or to investigate a security problem. If that is more trust than you want to extend, self-host: then we hold nothing at all.
