Calendar, visual pass

Every surface in docs/CALENDAR.md drawn against one week of fixture data: Monday October 5, 2026, 3:40pm. One rule carries the whole design: the more solid a block, the more real it is. Anything opaque blocks your time; anything you can see the hour lines through does not.

Appearance
L
Fill is the likelihood scaleSolid, striped, outlined, folded. Four steps of the same idea, so the bands read as an order without a legend. Ink never dims on a ghost: the words stay fully legible, only the container thins out. That is the difference between "not yet real" and "disabled".
Occlusion is busyReal events, likely ghosts, holds and estimated tails are opaque and hide the hour lines: they count against your free time. A maybe ghost is see-through: it is there to be noticed, not planned around.
Colour is the account, nothing elseTwo muted hues (sand for work, plum for personal) live only in fills. Blue stays what it is everywhere in Passband: state. It marks now, the next commitment, holds and suggested slots.

1. Sitrep agenda

Replaces the Calendar zone in the records rail. The next commitment first, today's shape as one strip, then the next few days. Most days this is the whole calendar. The scheduling card (6) is visible on the left, in For your eyes.

Next, then the shape, then the listThe callout answers "what do I have to be at", the strip answers "how full is today", the rows answer "what exactly". Earlier events today fold into "3 earlier": they are done, and the rail is short.
Unlikely stays out of the listThursday shows "3 unlikely" as a quiet line that expands in place. Squelch, applied to time: the VC dinner exists, it just does not get a row.
Automatic adds say so, onceThe Nopa confirmation arrived today, so its line at the foot of the zone says "added" with undo. After a day it is just an event.
Considered: the strip as the whole agendaA horizontal day strip per day (no rows) is beautiful and unreadable: you cannot tell a dentist from a standup without hovering. Kept one strip, for today only.
Considered: likelihood as a chip on every ghost rowThree chips per day made the list read like a status board. The swatch at the start of the row is the block in miniature and carries the band; the words "likely" and "maybe" sit faint at the end.

2. Day column

Thursday October 8, the busiest day in the fixtures. Real events solid, a likely invitation striped, a proposed meeting outlined, three unlikely invitations folded into the header, and a concert whose end is a guess.

(a) A ghost is an empty container, not a faded oneStriped or outlined, with full-strength ink and an evenly lit 1px edge. Nothing about it is greyed, so it never reads as a control you cannot press.
(c) The tail: known start, guessed body, no edgeOnly the first half hour is drawn solid, because only the start is a fact. The rest is the same hue, thinner, still opaque (it hides the hour lines, so it plainly blocks the time), and it has no bottom edge: it feathers out where the guess runs out. "ends ~11pm" sits just above the feather.
Night is folded, not cutMidnight to 7am is a thin strip at the top of every column. Anything there (the tail of Saturday's party, a 6am flight) still shows, and the grid keeps its hours for the day.
Considered: dashed outlines for ghostsDashes read as a drop target or an editor placeholder, and they break unevenly around a rounded corner. Rejected.
Considered: lower opacity for ghostsThe default, and exactly the trap: half-opaque text is how macOS draws a disabled control. Rejected.
Considered: a dotted or zig-zag bottom edge on the tailReads as a torn ticket, cute once. The feather says "about here" without inventing a shape.

3. Week

Seven day columns from the same renderer. Saturday's 10pm to 6am party is one block across two days: it leaves the bottom of Saturday with a flat edge and lands in Sunday's night strip with a flat top. Bills and deliveries sit in the all-day gutter as words, never as blocks.

(e) Two hues, low saturation, no borders on real eventsA week with eighteen work blocks and a dozen personal ones still reads as two quiet materials rather than confetti, because the hue only ever changes between accounts and never per calendar or per kind.
Corners tell the truth about edgesRounded corners only where an event really starts or ends. A flat edge means "continues": past midnight, or past the visible hours.
Considered: a 24-hour gridHonest, but two thirds of the scroll would be sleep. Considered a 6am-to-6am day as well; it puts 1am on Saturday under Friday, which is wrong the first time you book a red-eye.
Considered: per-calendar colours from GoogleThat is where confetti comes from: eight calendars, eight colours, none chosen by Passband. The privacy option (other accounts as busy only) would draw the other account as plain grey blocks with no titles.

4. Month: the shape of the month

No titles in the cells. Each day is a strip of its own waking hours, 7am to midnight left to right, with a folded night cap at its start. Because every strip shares the axis, a week row reads as one continuous timeline, and the month reads as a texture: where the evenings are full, which weeks are heavy, where you are away. Trips and stays are spans across the top. Selecting a day opens its column beside the month.

(d) Load is the amount of inkNo number, no heat colour. A full strip is a full day. The concert's feathered tail, the striped invitations and the outlined maybes all survive at this scale, so you can see what is likely without reading anything.
Midnight is the cell borderA strip ends at midnight and the next day's starts with its night cap, so Saturday's party runs off the end of Saturday and into the start of Sunday, and Halloween does the same into November 1.
Spans carry the only wordsLisbon and Tahoe are named, because a trip is the thing you plan the month around. Everything else is one click away in the column.
Considered: miniature vertical day columns per cellMatches the day view, but at month scale an hour is three pixels tall and an outlined ghost collapses into a line. Horizontal gives each hour seven pixels.
Considered: a heatmap, one tint per day by hours bookedTells you how much but not when. "Every evening this week is gone" is the useful sentence, and a heatmap cannot say it.

5. Event detail

Two events from Thursday side by side. The concert is a commitment with an estimated end; the VC dinner is an unlikely invitation with its reason. Both list the mail behind them, which is the event's index into the mailbox.

The tilde is a different colour, not just a characterEvery estimate is the dim ink and carries "~", and the one line under the time says where the guess came from and how to fix it (drag the end).
(b) The band names itself with its own swatch"Unlikely" beside the folded-count mark, "Likely" beside the stripes. The reason is one sentence of counting. No percentage, no meter, no three-step bar.
RSVP is the only verb on an invitationy and n answer through the calendar API. Nothing here writes or offers to write an email.

6. Scheduling card on the sitrep

Jonah asked "Thursday 2pm or Friday morning?". His row in For your eyes answers without the mail being opened. A number key takes a slot: it becomes a hold and the reply box opens, empty, with the cursor in it.

Each slot shows its afternoon, not just a verdictThe little strip is the hours around the slot, so "free" and "dentist 9 to 10" come with the shape of the rest of that morning.
The reply box is emptyThe only help on offer is the time itself: one key inserts "Thu Oct 8, 2:00pm PT". The sentence is the user's.
Considered: an "accept and reply" buttonOne click that writes "Thursday at 2 works" is exactly the generated prose the product refuses. Rejected by rule, not taste.

7. Time pane beside the composer

The user typed "we should meet at". The pane slides in beside the composer, not as a side panel, so the composer keeps the keys. It shows the next two working days and three suggested slots; Wednesday 11am is already held for Dana, so it is drawn as a hold and not offered again.

Suggestions are the only accent fills on the gridNumbered, so ⌘1 to ⌘3 insert without leaving the keyboard. Holds use the striped soft-busy mark in the accent, because a hold is Passband's own state, not an account's event.
The recipient's clock is beside each slot"5:00pm for Sam" comes from the Date headers of Sam's past mail. It is a hint for the user, not part of the inserted text.
Considered: inline autocomplete in the editorGhost text after the caret is how every "write this for me" feature starts. A pane with a click is unmistakably "insert this fact".

Where the spec was awkward to draw

"Committed ghosts render as solid ghosts" before the grantVisually a solid ghost is a real event, so the only difference left is provenance. Every event born in mail carries the small envelope mark; before the grant that mark is all that separates "Passband knows" from "your calendar knows". Worth deciding whether that is enough.
What counts as busy for a maybe§3.5 says likely is soft-busy and unlikely is not, and is silent on maybe. Drawn here as not busy (see-through). If maybe should count, it needs to become opaque, which collapses it into likely.
How much of an estimated event is "the known start"§3.4 says draw the known start solid. Only the start instant is known, so the solid cap is a convention (30 minutes here), not data.
"Friday morning" is a range, not a slotTaking it has to hold something concrete. Drawn as holding the free part, 10:15 to 12. The inserted text for a range is undefined.
Whose time zone is insertedThe doc's example inserts PT. When the pane knows Sam is on ET, inserting only PT pushes the arithmetic onto Sam, which is the thing §1 forbids for the user. Open: insert both?
Proposal candidates have no band of their ownJonah's two slots are drawn as maybe until one is taken. Proposals may want their own mark (linked, "1 of 2"); the fixtures got by with the label.
