Passband vs Slashy Checked October 2026

An inbox that writes back, or one that stays quiet.

Slashy is an AI email client that does more of your email for you. In its own words it “drafts replies in your tone, triages your inbox before you see it, and makes sure no follow-up slips,” with an agent you can text to search, schedule and send. Passband is a different idea. It reads every message as it arrives and tells you what needs you and why, so you open fewer emails, and it never writes your email for you. Sign-in codes get a sealed page of their own. The rest is squelched.

01 · OPEN LESS MAIL

Know what needs you.

Slashy sorts your inbox into tabs and has a reply waiting. Passband changes what the inbox asks of you. Every email still arrives. Passband reads each one, pulls forward the few that need you with a line saying why, and files the rest where you can find them when you want to.

Slashy

A label, and a tab for it.

Slashy uses AI to classify every incoming email into an importance label, and each label is a tab: Important, Calendar, Billing, Newsletter and Other out of the box. You can add labels described in your own words, and from:, to: and subject: filters that bypass AI entirely.

Important is for emails requiring your response. When one lands in the wrong tab you move it, and your past corrections improve accuracy.

Passband

A judgment, with its reason.

Triage decides, thread by thread, whether something needs you, what it asks of you and by when, and records why. Bills, packages and appointments come out of the mail as records instead of sitting in a tab. Your sender rules reach it as instructions in your own words.

It is a model's judgment, so it will sometimes be wrong. Press v and that email is fixed, and the correction is kept. Press t and you change how that sender is handled from now on.

When the model cannot be reached, new mail waits to be judged rather than being filed on a guess. A correction you make by hand stands: triage does not overrule it.

What that is worth.

Estimate About 1.6 hours a week

About 290 of your 350 emails a week needed nothing from you.

An estimate, not a promise. The share is measured and the seconds are yours to set. In one of our own mailboxes, 836 emails arrived between September 6 and October 7, 2026, and 695 of them, 83%, needed nothing from their owner: newsletters, receipts, shipping updates and noise. Your share will differ. Mail that does need you takes the same time either way, so it is not counted, and neither is any time you choose to spend in Reading.

02 · SIGN-IN CODES

Sign-in codes stay sealed.

Sign-in codes and password reset links do not sit in your inbox between a newsletter and a bill. They go to a page of their own, and they are kept out of search, out of Passband's assistant, and away from any agent you connect.

Passband decides which messages carry a code or a reset link, and a message it has not assessed yet stays closed to agents until it has. A routine “new sign-in to your account” notice with no code in it may still be readable. The full rules are in the threat model.

Slashy makes the opposite call, for convenience. Its help center offers an automation that “instantly forwards verification codes from email to iMessage,” in which “the agent extracts just the code number,” and its iPhone app can AutoFill emailed codes. Passband shows you a code the moment it lands too. The difference is that no agent is ever handed it.

03 · CUSTODY

Where your mail is kept.

Both products send mail to cloud models to do their work, and both say no one trains on it. The difference is where your mail is kept in the meantime, and who holds it.

Slashy

Stored with Slashy.

Slashy's help center lists what it stores: Email metadata and content, cached for fast access, with your calendar events, agent conversations and memories. Where: Encrypted servers in the United States, hosted on AWS with SOC 2 compliance.

It names Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Groq as AI providers, and gives their retention as 30-day logs, then deleted for Anthropic and OpenAI and none for the other two. On its own staff: No one reads your email unless you explicitly share it for debugging.

Its commitment, stated plainly: your mail is never used to train any AI model, not ours, not our providers'. And it has the paperwork: SOC 2 Type II, Google's CASA Tier 2, quarterly penetration tests.

Passband

Your machine, or an instance of your own.

Self-host and Passband runs on your own Mac, NAS or server. Your mail, search index and Google tokens live on your disk; we hold no copy and no key. Triage uses an Anthropic or OpenAI key you supply, under your own account.

Hosted, your account is its own isolated instance with its own database. Your Google token is sealed to a key only your instance holds, and backups are encrypted before they leave the server. Triage reaches Anthropic through our gateway, which keeps no bodies. Training on your mail: never, not by us, not by our model provider.

We do not have a SOC 2 report yet, and the people who run Passband can, in principle, reach a hosted mailbox. The trust page says so, and names every subprocessor and how long each keeps what.

04 · YOUR AGENT

Your agent, briefed.

Both connect to AI agents over MCP, and they make opposite bets. Slashy's server hands your agent Slashy's own toolset: it can “draft, send, reply, schedule, label, and attach files,” and it reaches your calendar, contact research and automations too. Passband's Agent MCP hands your agent what triage already worked out: what needs you and why, what is due, what is on its way. It writes mail only as far as you allow: out of the box a draft you send yourself, and sending only if you turn it on. Sign-in codes never reach it. It is available on self-host today and rolling out to hosted.

06 · WHICH ONE

Who should pick which.

Pick Slashy

If you want it done for you.

You send a lot of mail and you want a first draft of every reply, an agent you can text, and your calendar and CRM in the same window.

  • You use Outlook or Microsoft 365, or want your mail in a browser.
  • You work with a team: comments, assigned threads, a CRM.
  • You want AI to draft in your voice and chase your follow-ups.
  • Your company needs a SOC 2 report before it signs.
Pick Passband

If you would rather open less.

You want to know what needs you without reading everything to find out, you write your own email, and you want your mail in your custody.

  • You use Gmail, on a Mac and an iPhone.
  • Bills, packages, newsletters and sign-in codes bury the mail that matters.
  • You want to run it yourself, or have an instance of your own.
  • You want to hand your agent a briefing, not your mailbox.

Passband is early. It is Gmail only, the iPhone app is on TestFlight, and hosted is an invitation-only preview. Slashy's costs are real too, and its own help center is candid about them: the trial needs a card, a handed-off goal emails people without asking each time, and it lists as a known issue that “sometimes the agent says ‘I've sent the email’ when it actually created a draft.”
